ABOUT THE PROJECT

Why ACUMEN exists.

Students are constantly given advice about motivation, focus and productivity. Much of it is generic. ACUMEN tries a different loop: observe behavior, make a cautious interpretation, test an intervention, then look at what happened.

Is ACUMEN a psychological diagnosis?

No. It is a self-report educational tool. Its internal vectors are designed for product guidance, not clinical assessment or validated psychometric measurement.

Why Likert instead of A/B/C/D?

Forced-choice answers can hide degree. A five-point scale lets a student say “sometimes” or “very often”, while keeping the response simple enough for repeated scans.

Why does ACUMEN save history?

Because a single snapshot is easy to overinterpret. Longitudinal history lets students compare contexts and test whether a strategy coincided with a meaningful change.

What research informs the advice?

The learning layer draws on evidence around retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving and metacognitive monitoring. The motivation layer uses self-determination theory concepts such as autonomy, competence and relatedness. The app deliberately avoids turning these frameworks into personality labels.

Who made this?

Students. ACUMEN is intentionally built around the “by students, for students” principle: practical, skeptical of productivity theatre, and designed to improve through feedback.
