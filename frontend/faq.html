ABOUT ACUMEN

A little more honest than most study apps.

ACUMEN is built around a simple idea: before telling you how to study, it should help you notice how you actually study. The profile is a starting point, not a verdict. Then you test something, see what happens, and adjust.

Is ACUMEN a psychological diagnosis?

No. The scan is a self-report educational tool. Its scores are directional signals created for ACUMEN, not a clinical assessment and not a validated test for diagnosis, admissions or personality typing.

So should I trust my profile?

Treat it as a useful hypothesis. Your answers describe how you see yourself in the context of the questions, and that can change with workload, environment and experience. If a result feels wrong, that is information too.

Why does ACUMEN give me advice based on my profile?

The point is not to give everyone the same productivity checklist. ACUMEN uses the lower-actionable signals in your scan to suggest small experiments, drawing on established learning ideas such as retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving and metacognitive monitoring.

Will the AI always get it right?

No. AI can misunderstand you, make a bad suggestion or simply sound more confident than it deserves to. That is why ACUMEN keeps the profile separate from diagnosis and treats AI output as something to examine, not an authority.

Why does ACUMEN ask me to experiment?

Because knowing a description of yourself is not the same thing as knowing what helps you. A small test gives you something concrete to compare with your actual experience.

Do I need an account?

No. You can use ACUMEN locally without creating one. An account is mainly useful if you want your assessment history to follow you across devices through Supabase.

What does the Gemini key do?

The same Gemini API key powers ACUMEN’s generative features, including Mr. Brown, Quiz Forge and Study Plan. The scan and its local blueprint do not depend on Gemini.

What research ideas sit behind the learning advice?

The learning layer draws on research-informed mechanisms including retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, worked examples and metacognitive monitoring. The motivation layer uses concepts from Self-Determination Theory, such as autonomy, competence and relatedness. ACUMEN does not turn these ideas into fixed personality labels.

Who is ACUMEN for?

Students who want to understand their own study habits a little better and do something with that information. It is deliberately practical, but it is also deliberately skeptical of perfect schedules and miracle techniques. Humans have survived this long without another app telling them to wake up at 5 a.m.
